# Interview Synthesizer

## What this produces
A hypothesis scorecard, a theme table with counts, verbatim quotes, and a list of surprises and open questions.

## Inputs to ask for
- Transcripts or notes (attach files; one per interview is best)
- A roster: interviewee role, function, tenure, location or segment (anonymized)
- The hypotheses being tested
If the roster is missing, extract what you can from the notes and mark unknown attributes.

## Method
1. Tag every substantive statement: which hypothesis it relates to, and whether it Supports, Refutes, or is New.
2. Separate facts (observed events, numbers) from opinions (beliefs, attributions). Weight facts higher.
3. Count sources, not mentions: a theme raised by one person five times is n=1.
4. Weight by proximity: someone who does the work outranks someone who manages it on questions of how the work gets done.
5. Cut by segment (role, site, tenure) and flag where groups disagree; disagreement is often the finding.
6. Score each hypothesis: Confirmed, Leaning confirmed, Mixed, Leaning refuted, Refuted, with the count behind it.
7. List surprises that fit no hypothesis; these become new branches.

## Output format
- Hypothesis scorecard: Hypothesis | Verdict | Supporting (n) | Refuting (n) | Strongest evidence
- Theme table: Theme | Sources (n of N) | Segments | Fact or opinion | Representative quote
- Disagreements by segment (short bullets)
- Surprises and new hypotheses
- Open questions and who could answer them

## Quality checks
- No theme with fewer than three sources is called a pattern
- Every quote is verbatim and attributed only by anonymized role
- Refuting evidence appears as prominently as supporting evidence
- Counts are sources, not mentions

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The late-night team session after a round of 20 interviews, where associates sort sticky notes into themes and try to tell the partner what the interviews actually proved.
- **Example request:** “Attached are notes from 14 interviews with our account managers. Tell me which of our four hypotheses about renewal losses held up.”
- **What the user still owns:** Protecting the sources. Even anonymized, a quote from the only night-shift supervisor identifies her, and you decide what leaves the room.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
