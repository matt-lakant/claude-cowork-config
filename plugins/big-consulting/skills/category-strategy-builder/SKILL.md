# Category Strategy Builder

## What this produces
A category profile, its Kraljic position with rationale, a sourcing strategy, savings levers with ranges, and a 12-month action plan.

## Inputs to ask for
- 12 to 24 months of spend for the category by supplier, item, and business unit (attach CSV)
- Current contracts: term, expiry, pricing mechanism
- Business requirements and switching constraints (qualification, tooling, regulation)
- Supply market facts: credible supplier count, capacity, price trends
If supply market facts are missing, list the questions to answer and mark positions provisional.

## Method
1. Profile spend: total, supplier count, share of top three suppliers, price trend, spend under contract.
2. Score profit impact (spend size, effect on product cost or quality) and supply risk (supplier count, switching cost, scarcity) on 1 to 5.
3. Place it on Peter Kraljic's matrix (Harvard Business Review, 1983): leverage (high impact, low risk), strategic (high, high), bottleneck (low impact, high risk), non-critical (low, low).
4. Match strategy to quadrant: leverage gets competitive tender and volume consolidation; strategic gets partnership and joint cost reduction; bottleneck gets supply assurance and redesign away from the constraint; non-critical gets catalogs, automation, and fewer suppliers.
5. List savings levers with a range: price, demand, specification, payment terms, total cost of ownership.
6. Build the plan around contract expiry dates.

## Output format
- Category profile table (spend, suppliers, top-3 share, contract coverage, trend)
- Kraljic position with two-sentence rationale
- Lever table: Lever | Savings range | Effort | Risk
- 12-month plan: Month | Action | Owner

## Quality checks
- Scores cite the facts behind them
- Savings are ranges tied to the addressable spend base
- Bottleneck categories include a supply-risk action

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The category strategy phase of a sourcing wave: consultants profiling spend, supply market, and internal needs, then writing a strategy per category.
- **Example request:** “Here’s two years of our corrugated packaging spend. Build a category strategy before the main contract renews in March.”
- **What the user still owns:** The internal customers. Engineering and operations choose specs and suppliers too, and a strategy they did not help write will be worked around.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
