# Rolling Forecast Redesigner

## What this produces
A future-state planning design covering horizon, cadence, granularity, calendar, roles, and the link between forecasts, targets, and incentives, with a transition plan.

## Inputs to ask for
- The current budget and forecast calendar, with steps, dates, and participants
- Estimated hours spent per cycle by function
- How budgets link to incentives and spending approval
- Forecast accuracy history if available
If hours are unknown, estimate from participants and cycle length and label as estimate.

## Method
1. Map the current cycle: steps, iterations, and hours. Flag time spent negotiating rather than forecasting.
2. Set the horizon: typically five to six quarters rolling, so the next fiscal year is always visible.
3. Set cadence: monthly or quarterly updates, matched to how fast the business changes.
4. Set granularity: forecast at driver and summary-line level, well above the chart-of-accounts level of a traditional budget.
5. Separate the three numbers: target (what we aim for), forecast (what we think will happen), resource allocation (what we fund). Mixing them makes forecasts negotiating positions.
6. Define roles and a calendar: who updates which drivers, by which working day, reviewed in which meeting.
7. Plan the transition: run in parallel with the budget for two to three cycles, then shrink the annual budget.

## Output format
- Current vs future state table: Dimension | Today | Future
- Planning calendar for one cycle: Day | Activity | Owner
- Roles table: Role | Inputs owned | Review forum
- Transition plan by quarter

## Quality checks
- Targets, forecasts, and resource allocation are explicitly separated
- Hours saved are estimated from the current-state map
- The design names what happens to incentive targets
- Detail level is lower than today's budget

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A planning-process redesign: consultants mapping the annual budget calendar, counting the hours it consumes, and designing a rolling forecast that replaces or shrinks it.
- **Example request:** “Our budget takes four months and is wrong by spring. Here’s the calendar. Redesign it as a rolling forecast.”
- **What the user still owns:** The incentive question. As long as bonuses pay on the budget, people will sandbag the forecast, and only the executive team can change that.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
