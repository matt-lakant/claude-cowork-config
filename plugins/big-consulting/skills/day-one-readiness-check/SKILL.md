# Day 1 Readiness Check

## What this produces
A minimal Day 1 must-have list with owners and test evidence, a readiness score by area, go/no-go criteria, and a runbook for close day and the 72 hours after.

## Inputs to ask for
- Workstream Day 1 plans (attach them)
- Legal entity structure and close date
- Employee population by location and payroll provider
- Key systems both companies run
- Regulatory approvals and their conditions
If workstreams have no written plans, generate the default checklist and mark every item UNOWNED.

## Method
1. List candidate items by area. Legal: entities, licenses, signatory authority, bank accounts. People: payroll, benefits continuity, employment letters, badges. Customers: ordering, invoicing, contract notices. Suppliers: purchase orders, payments, vendor master. Finance: cash management, reporting. IT: email, network, access, security. Communications: who says what, when.
2. Sort each item into Day 1, Day 30, or Day 100. Day 1 holds only what would break the business or the law if missing.
3. For each Day 1 item: owner, status, test evidence (mock payroll run, test invoice, access dry run), and fallback.
4. Score readiness per area. Any Day 1 item without test evidence counts as not ready.
5. Set go/no-go criteria for close, agreed with the steering committee in advance.
6. Draft the runbook: hour-by-hour close day, a command center, an issue escalation path, and checkpoints at 24, 48, and 72 hours.

## Output format
- Table: Area | Item | Day 1/30/100 | Owner | Status | Test evidence | Fallback
- Readiness score by area
- Go/no-go criteria
- Close-day runbook

## Quality checks
- Payroll and customer invoicing both have test evidence
- The Day 1 list holds only items that would break the business or the law
- Every Day 1 item has an owner and a fallback

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The Day 1 checklist and readiness assessment, where every workstream certifies that on the first day of combined ownership people get paid, customers get served, and legal requirements are met.
- **Example request:** “We close on the 1st. Here are the workstream plans. Tell me what’s not ready for Day 1 and build the close-day runbook.”
- **What the user still owns:** The go/no-go call. Delaying a close has contractual and political costs, and weighing those against a red payroll test is your decision.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
