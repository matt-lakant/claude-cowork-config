# Incentive Alignment Review

## What this produces
A metric-to-strategy alignment grid, a plan mechanics review, a pay-for-performance test, and redesign recommendations with modeled cost.

## Inputs to ask for
- Plan documents for each incentive plan (attach).
- The strategy priorities and company KPIs for the year.
- Payout history by participant or group for 2 to 3 years, with the results measured.
- If payout history is missing, review design only and label the performance test as not run.

## Method
1. List every metric in each plan with its weight. Map each to a strategy priority; flag unmapped metrics and uncovered priorities.
2. Test line of sight: can participants influence each metric through their own work? Flag metrics beyond their control.
3. Review payout curves: threshold, target, maximum, and slope (payout change per point of performance). Flag cliffs and uncapped plans.
4. Identify gaming risks: sandbagging targets, pulling revenue into the period, margin sacrificed for volume, conflicting metrics across teams that share work.
5. Test pay for performance: compare payout percentage against result percentage by year and group. Flag years where payouts exceeded target while results missed.
6. Recommend changes and model the cost at target and at maximum.

## Output format
1. Alignment grid: Plan | Metric | Weight | Strategy Priority | Line of Sight (Y/N).
2. Mechanics: Plan | Threshold | Target | Max | Slope | Issues.
3. Pay for performance: Year | Group | Result % of Target | Payout % of Target.
4. Recommendations with cost impact.

## Quality checks
- Every strategy priority is either covered by a metric or flagged.
- Cost is modeled at target and maximum.
- Conflicting metrics across teams are named explicitly.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A rewards review, when consultants test whether the bonus and commission plans actually pay for the behavior the strategy needs and model what the plans cost.
- **Example request:** “Our ops managers’ bonuses paid out at 115% last year while on-time delivery fell. Here are the plan documents and payout data. Tell me what the plan is really rewarding.”
- **What the user still owns:** Holding the new plan when the first payout disappoints someone important. Every plan redesign gets tested in the first quarter it bites.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
