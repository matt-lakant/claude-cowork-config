# Stakeholder Alignment Map

## What this produces
A stakeholder table, a dated pre-wiring calendar, a short brief for each one-on-one, and a go or postpone rule for the decision meeting.

## Inputs to ask for
- The proposal and the decision meeting date
- Names or roles of approvers, likely blockers, funders, and the people who must carry it out
- What the user knows of each person's view and history with the topic
- Who has influence over whom (who listens to whom)
Warn the user that the output is sensitive: label it confidential and keep it out of shared folders.

## Method
1. List everyone who can approve, block, fund, or must execute, plus indirect influencers (chief of staff, a trusted FP&A lead, a union steward).
2. Rate each: influence on this decision (High, Medium, Low), current stance (-2 opposed to +2 champion), and the stance required for the decision to pass and stick.
3. For each gap, diagnose one primary reason: economic (loses budget or headcount), control (loses decision rights), information (has not seen the facts), messenger (distrusts who is bringing it), or history (was burned by a similar effort).
4. Match approach to reason: facts for information gaps, a design change or trade for economic, a governance role for control, a different messenger for messenger, a pilot or safeguard for history.
5. Sequence: supporters first to sharpen the proposal, then high-influence undecided people, then likely opponents, all before the group meeting.
6. Write a brief per one-on-one: objective, what they care about, the one thing to learn from them, what we can offer or ask.
7. Set the go rule: 48 hours before the meeting, any High-influence person still at -1 or below means change the proposal or move the date.

## Output format
- Table: Role | Influence | Current | Required | Gap reason | Approach | Messenger | Meeting date
- Pre-wiring calendar
- One-on-one briefs, 100 words maximum each
- Go or postpone rule

## Quality checks
- Every approver and every person who must execute appears
- Every gap has a diagnosed reason next to its score
- No likely opponent first sees the proposal in the group meeting
- Stance ratings are labeled as the user's judgment

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The engagement manager’s private stakeholder grid and the calendar of one-on-one “pre-wiring” conversations that makes sure nobody in the final meeting hears the recommendation for the first time.
- **Example request:** “I need the COO, CFO, and three regional VPs behind moving scheduling into a shared service before the Q1 operating review. Build the alignment map and tell me who to see first.”
- **What the user still owns:** The relationships. The map tells you who to see and in what order; whether they trust you enough to change their mind was settled long before this decision.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
