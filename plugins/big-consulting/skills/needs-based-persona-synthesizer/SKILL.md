# Needs-Based Persona Synthesizer

## What this produces
A coded evidence table and 3 to 5 persona cards built on what buyers need and how they buy, with counts and verbatim quotes behind every claim.

## Inputs to ask for
- Transcripts or detailed notes (attach as text, DOCX, or PDF). Minimum 8; 15 to 25 is typical.
- For each interview: role, company size, customer status (current, lost, prospect), and date.
- Segment assignments from a quantitative segmentation, if one exists.

## Method
1. Code every transcript on seven fields: trigger (what started the search), goal, current workaround, top pains, decision criteria in rank order, buying process and people involved, deal-breakers.
2. Keep one verbatim quote per coded item, with interview ID.
3. Check the sample: count interviews by status and role. If more than 70% are current happy customers, say so and cap confidence at "directional."
4. Cluster interviews on needs, triggers, and decision criteria. Titles and company size are applied afterward as descriptors.
5. A persona requires at least 3 supporting interviews. Anything with fewer goes to an "emerging signals" list.
6. For each persona, name the tension that separates it from its nearest neighbor (for example, speed of deployment against control over configuration).
7. If segment data exists, map personas to segments.

## Output format
1. Coding table: Interview ID | Status | Trigger | Goal | Workaround | Top Pain | #1 Criterion | Deal-Breaker.
2. Persona cards (one per persona, under 200 words): name, interviews supporting (n), trigger, job they hire us for, top 3 criteria, buying process, what loses them, 2 quotes.
3. Emerging signals (fewer than 3 interviews).
4. Sample bias statement.

## Quality checks
- Every persona claim has an interview ID behind it.
- No quote is paraphrased inside quotation marks.
- No invented demographic detail (names, ages, hobbies).

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The synthesis week after twenty customer interviews, when a team codes every transcript on sticky notes and argues its way to four personas.
- **Example request:** “I’ve attached 18 win/loss interview transcripts from this year. Give me the distinct buyer personas and what each one actually cares about when choosing a vendor.”
- **What the user still owns:** Who got interviewed. If your team only called friendly accounts, the personas will flatter you, and no amount of synthesis fixes a biased sample.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
