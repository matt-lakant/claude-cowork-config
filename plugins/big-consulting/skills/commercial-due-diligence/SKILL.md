# Commercial Due Diligence Review

## What this produces
A growth decomposition, revenue-quality metrics, a claim-versus-evidence table, a bridge from the management case to a diligence case, and ranked red flags.

## Inputs to ask for
- The management plan or CIM projections
- Revenue by customer, product, and year for at least 3 years (attach the data room export)
- Pipeline and historical win rates
- Market sources the seller cites, and customer interview or reference call notes
If customer-level revenue is not available, say that revenue quality cannot be tested and list it as the first data request.

## Method
1. Decompose plan growth into market growth, share gain, price, new products, and acquisitions. Flag any plan where share gain and new products carry most of the growth.
2. Revenue quality: top-10 customer share and any customer above 10%, gross and net revenue retention by cohort, price versus volume split, recurring versus one-time.
3. Test the market growth assumption against at least two independent sources.
4. Code customer interviews for satisfaction, switching intent, share of wallet, and reasons for choosing the seller. Compare with management's claims.
5. Pipeline: coverage of next year's plan and historical conversion versus the rate the plan assumes.
6. Build the diligence case by resetting each driver to the evidence, then bridge management case to diligence case.
7. Translate red flags into deal terms: price, earnout, representations, or walk away.

## Output format
- Growth decomposition table
- Revenue quality metrics table
- Table: Management claim | Evidence | Verdict (supported / partial / unsupported)
- Revenue bridge, management case to diligence case, by driver
- Red flags ranked by revenue impact, with the deal implication

## Quality checks
- Every metric traces to the data room files
- The diligence case changes only drivers with evidence behind the change
- Interview findings state how many customers said each thing

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The commercial diligence workstream: customer calls, a market model, and a report that tests whether the seller’s revenue plan holds up.
- **Example request:** “Here’s the CIM and the customer revenue file from the data room for the software target. Tell me whether their 18% growth plan holds and what our case should be.”
- **What the user still owns:** What you will pay for the gap. How much of it to price in is a negotiation call.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
