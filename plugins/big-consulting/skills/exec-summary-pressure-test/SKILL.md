# Executive Summary Pressure Test

## What this produces
A seven-test scorecard with quoted evidence, a revised summary, and the three questions the summary still leaves open.

## Inputs to ask for
- The draft executive summary
- The message the author intends the reader to take away, in one sentence
- The supporting document or deck, so numbers can be checked (attach it)
- Who will read it and what they will do with it
If the intended message is not given, run the cold read first and ask the author whether it matches.

## Method
1. Cold read: restate what a reader with 60 seconds would take away, in two sentences. Compare with the intended message. A mismatch is a fail regardless of other scores.
2. Answer first: the recommendation or central conclusion must be in the first two sentences.
3. So-what: ask "so what?" of every sentence. Sentences that describe activity ("we analyzed," "the team interviewed") or leave the implication unstated fail.
4. Numbers: for each figure, confirm source, period, unit, and match with the body. Recalculate every derived number. Check rounding is consistent.
5. Logic: each supporting point must support the conclusion. Flag leaps, and flag supports that overlap.
6. Missing pieces: check for cost, risk, timing, owner, and the specific ask. Name what the toughest reader would ask first.
7. Language: flag stacked hedges ("may potentially"), undefined jargon, passive voice that hides who acts, and sentences over 30 words.
8. Score each test Pass, Partial, or Fail, then revise.

## Output format
- Scorecard table: Test | Result | Evidence (exact quote) | Fix
- Revised summary, same length or shorter
- Three open questions the revised summary still does not answer

## Quality checks
- Every Fail quotes the exact failing text
- The revision adds no fact that is not in the inputs
- The revision is no longer than the original
- Every recalculated number is shown with its arithmetic

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The partner review the night before a client meeting, where someone who has not seen the work reads the executive summary cold and finds every place it breaks.
- **Example request:** “Pressure test this executive summary for the procurement transformation readout. The point I want them to take away is that we should fund phase two now.”
- **What the user still owns:** Which critiques to accept. The test finds the soft spots; whether hedging a claim is prudence or timidity is a call about your audience and your evidence.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
