# Pyramid Storyline Builder

## What this produces
A one-page storyline: SCQA opening, governing thought, 3 to 5 key-line arguments, and the evidence under each, ready to become slide titles or memo headings.

## Inputs to ask for
- The audience and the decision or reaction wanted from them
- The analysis: draft deck, model outputs, interview notes, or a findings dump (attach the files)
- Any answer the author already believes, and known audience objections
- The day-one answer or working hypothesis from the start of the project, if one exists
If the audience decision is missing, ask once; if still missing, draft for "approve the recommendation" and flag it.

## Method
1. Write Situation (what the audience already accepts), Complication (what changed or is at risk), and Question (the one the audience would ask next). Nothing in the Situation should be disputable.
2. Write the governing thought as the answer to that Question: one sentence, under 25 words, with a verb and a consequence. "Our margin problem" fails. "Exit the two loss-making regions to recover 3 points of margin by FY27" passes.
3. Choose key-line logic. Default to inductive grouping (parallel reasons, actions, or options). Use deductive (statement, comment, therefore) only when the audience must follow a chain to accept the conclusion.
4. Draft 3 to 5 key-line points, each a full-sentence claim that answers "why?" or "how?" about the governing thought.
5. If a day-one answer exists, compare it with this storyline and state which claims survived, changed, or died; the audience often remembers the early version.
6. MECE test the key line: no two points overlap, together they fully support the governing thought, and one category word (reasons, steps, segments) names them all.
7. Under each point, place the specific evidence from the inputs: number, source, and the chart it becomes. Mark gaps EVIDENCE NEEDED.
8. Order points by importance for a decision, time for a plan, structure for a diagnosis.

## Output format
- SCQA block (4 lines)
- Governing thought (1 sentence, bold)
- Table: # | Key-line claim | Supporting evidence | Source | Gap?
- Weakest link: the key-line point most likely to be challenged, and why (2 sentences)
Maximum one page.

## Quality checks
- Reading only the governing thought and key line tells the whole story
- Every key-line point is a claim with a verb, never a topic label
- Each evidence cell names a source from the inputs or says EVIDENCE NEEDED
- No key-line point restates another in different words

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The storyline session near the end of an engagement, where the engagement manager stands at a whiteboard and forces a pile of analysis into one governing thought and three supporting arguments.
- **Example request:** “Here’s the analysis on consolidating the two Midwest plants and my notes from last week’s ops review. Build me a pyramid storyline for the October board.”
- **What the user still owns:** Choosing the answer you will defend in the room. The skill will structure any conclusion you hand it; only you know which one the business can execute.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
