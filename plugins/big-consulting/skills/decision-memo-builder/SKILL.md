# Decision Memo Builder

## What this produces
A decision memo of two pages or less with a scoring matrix, sensitivity result, recommendation, and dated review points.

## Inputs to ask for
- The decision, its deadline, and the single person who owns it
- Options already on the table and any analysis behind them (attach models or decks)
- The criteria leadership cares about, and their relative weight
- Constraints: budget, timing, capability, contractual
- Known dissent and the reasons for it
If weights are not given, propose them and get the user to confirm before scoring.

## Method
1. Frame the decision as one question with a deadline and one owner.
2. Build at least three genuinely different options, always including the status quo and a staged or hybrid option. Drop any option no competent person would choose.
3. Fix 4 to 6 criteria (for example value, cost, risk, time to impact, capability fit, reversibility) with weights summing to 100, confirmed before any scoring. Changing weights after seeing scores is a red flag; note it if it happens.
4. Score each option 1 to 5 per criterion with a one-line rationale tied to evidence. Compute weighted totals.
5. Sensitivity: move each weight by 10 points and report whether the winner changes. If it does, name the criterion the decision really turns on.
6. Rate reversibility. Easy-to-reverse choices deserve a fast decision and a review date; hard-to-reverse ones deserve the deeper analysis.
7. Recommend: the option, why, what is given up, and the conditions under which the runner-up would be the better choice.
8. Set review points: leading indicators, kill criteria, and dates. Record dissent in the dissenter's own terms.

## Output format
Decision, owner, deadline | Recommendation (3 sentences) | Options table | Scoring matrix (weights, scores, rationale, totals) | Sensitivity result | Trade-offs | What would change our mind | Review points and kill criteria | Dissent.

## Quality checks
- The status quo is scored honestly, including what it costs to keep
- Weights were set before scores
- The sensitivity result is reported even when inconvenient
- The recommendation states what is given up

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The options paper an engagement team writes when leadership is split: three to five options scored against agreed criteria, a clear recommendation, and the conditions that would change it.
- **Example request:** “We’re split on whether to rebuild our customer portal in-house, buy a platform, or extend the current one another two years. Build me a decision memo I can take to the CEO Friday.”
- **What the user still owns:** The decision. A weighted matrix makes the trade-offs visible and still leaves them to you, and the owner named on page one has to sign it.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
