# S&OP Process Designer

## What this produces
A monthly S&OP calendar, a step-by-step design with inputs and outputs, agendas for each meeting, a metric set, and a maturity gap list.

## Inputs to ask for
- How demand and supply plans are made today, by whom, and when
- The planning horizon and product families
- Current pain points: forecast bias, stockouts, excess, expedites
- Participants available at the executive level
If the current process is unknown, interview the user through one month of how plans get made.

## Method
1. Set the five-step monthly cycle: data gathering, demand review, supply review, reconciliation (pre-meeting), executive meeting.
2. For each step define owner, inputs, outputs, and working day. Demand review produces a consensus unconstrained forecast; supply review produces a constrained plan and capacity gaps.
3. Plan at product family level over an 18 to 24 month horizon. SKU detail belongs in execution planning.
4. Define reconciliation rules: which gaps are resolved at pre-meeting and which escalate, with dollar or volume thresholds.
5. Build the executive agenda around decisions: gaps, scenarios, resource trade-offs.
6. Set metrics: forecast accuracy and bias, plan adherence, service level, inventory days.

## Output format
- Calendar: Working day | Step | Owner | Output
- Step design table: Step | Inputs | Outputs | Decisions | Participants
- Executive agenda (60 minutes, timed)
- Metric set with formulas
- Gaps between today and the design

## Quality checks
- Every step has one owner and a dated output
- Every executive agenda item ends in a decision
- Bias is measured separately from accuracy

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** An S&OP implementation: consultants designing the monthly cycle of demand review, supply review, reconciliation, and executive meeting, with inputs, owners, and decision rules for each step.
- **Example request:** “Sales and the plants never agree on the forecast and we have stockouts and excess at once. Design an S&OP process for our six product families.”
- **What the user still owns:** Making the executive meeting happen every month with the right people in the room. S&OP fails when leaders delegate it.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
