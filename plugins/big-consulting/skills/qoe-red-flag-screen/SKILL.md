# Quality of Earnings Red-Flag Screen

## What this produces
Reclassified EBITDA adjustments, trend flags, a working capital analysis, debt-like items, and seller questions ranked by value impact. A screen ahead of the formal report.

## Inputs to ask for
- Monthly P&L and balance sheet for 24 to 36 months, plus trial balances (attach the files)
- The seller's reported-to-adjusted EBITDA bridge
- Revenue recognition policy, AR and AP agings, audit reports and management letters
- The headline deal multiple
If only annual figures exist, run the adjustment review and state that trend tests could not be run.

## Method
1. Rebuild the EBITDA bridge. Classify each adjustment: supportable non-recurring, pro forma run-rate, owner-related, or questionable. Flag "one-time" categories that appear in more than one year.
2. Monthly trend tests: revenue spikes in the last month of a quarter, margin jumps in the trailing 12 months, reserve releases, rising capitalized costs.
3. Revenue recognition: bill-and-hold, side agreements, and swings in deferred revenue.
4. Working capital: net working capital each month, trailing-12-month average as the peg candidate, and moves before close such as stretched payables or accelerated collections.
5. Debt-like items: unpaid bonuses, customer deposits, deferred capex, aged payables, litigation, underfunded obligations.
6. Cash test: EBITDA-to-operating-cash conversion by year, and bank deposits against reported revenue.
7. Rank each flag by value impact: EBITDA effect times the deal multiple.

## Output format
- Table: Adjustment | Seller amount | Category | Our view | Adjusted amount | Evidence needed
- Red flags ranked by value impact
- Monthly working capital table with peg candidate
- Debt-like items list and seller question list

## Quality checks
- Every figure ties to a trial balance or source file
- Value impact is shown as arithmetic
- Output is labeled as a screen for qualified accountants to confirm

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The first pass a financial diligence team makes through the seller’s adjusted EBITDA, monthly financials, and working capital before the formal quality of earnings report comes back.
- **Example request:** “The seller says adjusted EBITDA is USD 22M on USD 17M reported. Here are the monthly financials and their bridge. Screen it and tell me which add-backs I should fight.”
- **What the user still owns:** The accounting opinion belongs to qualified accountants. How hard to push on each finding, given the seller relationship, belongs to you.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
