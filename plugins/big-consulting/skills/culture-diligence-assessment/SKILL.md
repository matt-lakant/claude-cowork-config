# Culture Diligence Assessment

## What this produces
A dimension-by-dimension culture comparison with evidence, gap and impact ratings tied to the deal thesis, flight-risk groups, and an intervention plan.

## Inputs to ask for
- The deal thesis
- Employee survey results for either company, if available
- Delegation-of-authority or approval policies, compensation plan structures, promotion criteria, remote-work policy
- Interview notes with leaders and employees
- Attrition data by group; public employee reviews the user pastes in
If documents are unavailable, run on interview notes alone and mark every rating LOW CONFIDENCE.

## Method
1. Compare on behaviors that leave evidence: decision making (central or distributed), approval thresholds, risk tolerance, pace, how performance is measured and paid, communication norms, work location.
2. Weight artifacts above stated values. An approval limit, a commission plan, or a promotion rule outranks a values poster.
3. Rate each gap low, medium, or high. Rate its impact on the deal thesis: if the thesis depends on cross-sell, a gap in sales compensation is high impact.
4. Name flight-risk groups by role and location where changes will land hardest. Never name individuals.
5. For each high-impact gap, recommend preserve, harmonize, or blend, with timing and the early decision it requires.
6. Define watch metrics for the integration: attrition by group, survey items, time to decision.

## Output format
- Table: Dimension | Company A evidence | Company B evidence | Gap | Deal impact | Recommendation
- Flight-risk groups table
- Watch metrics
- Two-paragraph summary for the deal team

## Quality checks
- Every rating cites at least one piece of evidence
- No individual is named
- Recommendations cover every high-impact gap

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The culture workstream of diligence: surveys, interviews, and a document review that compares how two companies really make decisions, to predict where integration will cause friction.
- **Example request:** “We’re buying a founder-run engineering firm that works fully remote and pays on project profit. Here are both comp plans and my interview notes. Where will culture bite us?”
- **What the user still owns:** Which culture wins where. The assessment shows the gaps and their cost; choosing whose way becomes the company’s way is the leadership decision everyone will remember.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
