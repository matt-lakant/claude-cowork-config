# RFP Builder

## What this produces
An RFP document, a pricing template that forces comparable bids, a weighted scoring model, and an event calendar.

## Inputs to ask for
- Scope of goods or services, locations, and contract term
- Historical volumes by item or service line (attach CSV)
- Mandatory requirements (certifications, insurance, service levels, data security)
- Evaluation priorities and who sits on the evaluation team
If volumes are missing, use ranges and state that suppliers must price at each tier.

## Method
1. Write scope precisely: what is in, what is out, service levels with numbers.
2. Split requirements into mandatory (pass/fail) and scored.
3. Build a pricing template with line-item unit prices, volume tiers, and separate fields for fees, freight, and escalation, so no supplier can bundle costs out of view.
4. Set weights before bids arrive, commonly price 40 to 60%, with capability, service, risk, and transition making up the rest.
5. Write a scoring rubric of 1 to 5 with a description of each score, so evaluators score consistently.
6. Set the timeline: release, questions deadline, answers published to all bidders, submission, shortlist, presentations, award.

## Output format
- RFP document outline with full text for scope and requirements
- Pricing template: Item | Unit | Annual volume | Unit price | Tier breaks | Fees | Escalation basis
- Scoring model: Criterion | Weight | Rubric for 1, 3, 5
- Timeline table

## Quality checks
- Weights total 100% and are fixed before release
- Every mandatory requirement is verifiable
- The pricing template leaves no room for hidden fees
- Questions and answers go to all bidders

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** Sourcing analysts drafting the RFP package: requirements, volumes, pricing templates, evaluation criteria, and the event timeline, usually adapted from a template that fits the category badly.
- **Example request:** “We’re rebidding janitorial services across 14 sites. Volumes attached. Build the RFP and a pricing sheet that lets us compare bids line by line.”
- **What the user still owns:** Keeping the event fair. The moment someone tips off the incumbent, the competitive tension is gone.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
