# Post-Implementation Review

## What this produces
A two-page review: outcome scorecard, variance root causes, lessons converted into changes with owners, and a close or continue decision on open items.

## Inputs to ask for
- Charter and business case
- Baseline and actual schedule, budget, and scope changes
- Benefits realized to date against ramp
- RAID history, post-go-live incident log
- Stakeholder survey results or interview notes
If stakeholder input is missing, draft a five-question survey for the user to send before the review.

## Method
1. Score objectives: each charter objective as met, partly met, or not met, with evidence.
2. Compute variances: schedule in working days, cost in dollars and percent, scope added and removed, benefits against ramp.
3. Pick the three largest variances and run five whys on each, stopping at a cause the organization controls.
4. Group findings: estimation, decision speed, vendor management, data, change management, governance.
5. Write each lesson as a specific change: what the next program does differently, who updates the playbook, by when.
6. Decide open items: remaining backlog, hypercare exit, benefit handover to the business owner.
7. Keep the language blameless: describe decisions and conditions, never individuals.

## Output format
- Scorecard: Objective | Target | Outcome | Status | Evidence
- Variance table: Dimension | Plan | Actual | Variance | Root cause
- Lessons: Lesson | Evidence | Change to make | Owner | Due
- Open items with close or continue decision
Two pages maximum.

## Quality checks
- Each lesson cites evidence from the inputs
- Each lesson produces a change with an owner
- No individual is named as a cause
- Benefits handover has a named business owner

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The lessons-learned exercise run 60 to 90 days after go-live, which usually produces a list nobody reads before the next program.
- **Example request:** “Our CRM rollout went live 75 days ago. Here’s the charter, final budget, schedule history, and incident log. Run the post-implementation review.”
- **What the user still owns:** Making the next program use it. Lessons change nothing until someone rewrites the playbook and enforces it at the next charter.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
