# Decision Rights Mapper

## What this produces
A decision inventory, a role chart for each critical decision, a list of collisions and gaps, and escalation rules.

## Inputs to ask for
- The org chart or structure in scope.
- Recent decisions that stalled or got reversed, with a sentence on what happened.
- Approval policies and delegation-of-authority limits (attach if they exist).
- Whether the user prefers RAPID or RACI. Default to RAPID for decisions, RACI for execution work.

## Method
1. List 15 to 30 recurring decisions that drive value (pricing exceptions, hiring above plan, capital requests, vendor selection). Exclude routine approvals.
2. Rate each on value at stake and frequency; the top third are critical.
3. For each critical decision, assign roles. RAPID (Rogers and Blenko at Bain): Recommend, Agree (veto, used sparingly), Perform, Input, Decide. RACI: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
4. Enforce one D (or one A) per decision. Flag any decision where two people believe they hold it.
5. Flag gaps: decisions with no owner, Agree roles on more than half of decisions, and decisions escalated more than two levels above where the information sits.
6. Write escalation rules: the trigger, the time limit, and who breaks the tie.

## Output format
1. Decision inventory: Decision | Value at Stake | Frequency | Critical (Y/N).
2. Role chart: Decision | R | A | P | I | D (or R | A | C | I).
3. Collisions and gaps with the evidence.
4. Escalation rules (under 8).

## Quality checks
- Every critical decision has exactly one decider.
- Roles are assigned to positions so the chart survives turnover.
- Agree (veto) roles are justified by regulation or material risk.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The decision-rights workstream of a reorg, when a team interviews leaders about who really decides, finds the collisions, and writes a RACI or RAPID chart everyone signs.
- **Example request:** “Pricing exceptions keep bouncing between sales, finance, and me. Map who should decide what on our 20 biggest commercial decisions and show me where we collide.”
- **What the user still owns:** Enforcing the chart when someone senior ignores it. Decision rights hold only if you back the decider in the first contested case.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
