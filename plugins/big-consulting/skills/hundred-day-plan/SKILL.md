# First 100 Days Plan

## What this produces
A 100-day plan with a critical-decisions calendar, retention and customer lists, quick wins, dated milestones, and a scorecard.

## Inputs to ask for
- The deal thesis and value targets
- Org charts, and any list of critical roles or people
- Top customers of the acquired business by revenue
- Integration approach by function, if set
- Known risks: poaching, contract renewals, system deadlines
If critical people are not identified, ask the user to name them before drafting the retention section.

## Method
1. State 3 to 5 outcomes the first 100 days must set up, each traced to the deal thesis.
2. Split the work into four tracks: stabilize (keep customers and key people), quick wins (visible results inside 100 days), foundations (org design decided, systems path chosen), and value capture launched.
3. Build the critical-decisions calendar: leadership team, org structure, brand, systems direction, site plan, each with a date and owner. Undated decisions are where integrations stall.
4. Retention: critical people, the retention mechanism for each, and a conversation with a senior leader in the first two weeks.
5. Customer outreach: top accounts called by a named executive by a set date, with what not to promise.
6. Pick 3 to 5 quick wins: visible, low risk, meaningful to employees or customers.
7. Set milestones at day 30, 60, and 100 with owners and metrics: value captured, customer retention, attrition against baseline.

## Output format
- Outcomes (5 lines maximum)
- Critical-decisions calendar: Decision | Owner | Date | Inputs needed
- Retention table and customer outreach table (roles and accounts, anonymized in shared copies)
- Milestone plan and scorecard

## Quality checks
- Every milestone traces to a stated outcome
- Every critical decision has a date inside the 100 days
- Retention conversations for critical people fall in the first 14 days

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The 100-day integration plan leadership presents after close: the decisions, retention moves, customer outreach, and value milestones.
- **Example request:** “We close on the packaging acquisition next month. Build the 100-day plan, and make sure we don’t lose their top 20 customers or their plant managers.”
- **What the user still owns:** Making the hard decisions on time. Making the org call that disappoints half the room on day 30 instead of day 90 is leadership.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
