# Industry Value Chain Mapper

## What this produces
A stage-by-stage map with players, concentration, typical margins, and control points, plus a short read on where the chain is shifting.

## Inputs to ask for
- Industry, geography, and the user's role in it
- 10-Ks or annual reports of two or three players at different stages (attach PDFs)
- Expert-call notes, association reports, or internal studies
If filings are missing, work from public knowledge and mark every margin as an estimate.

## Method
1. Define 5 to 8 stages from raw input to end use and aftermarket. Each stage must be an economic activity someone could own alone.
2. Per stage: activities, representative players, rough concentration, gross and operating margin from pure-play filings, capital intensity, and what the next stage pays for.
3. Trace three flows: product, money (including rebates, fees, pass-throughs), and customer information. The stage that owns customer data often owns the relationship.
4. Mark control points: capacity bottlenecks, standards or certifications, licenses, installed base, proprietary data.
5. Note which players span several stages and whether integration is rising or unwinding.
6. Flag stages being bundled, unbundled, or bypassed (direct-to-customer, platforms, insourcing).
7. Place the user and name the adjacent stage with the most power over them.

## Output format
- Mermaid flowchart, left to right, one node per stage
- Table: Stage | Activities | Example players | Concentration | Gross margin | Operating margin | Capital intensity | Control point
- Shifts: 3 to 5 bullets with evidence
- User position: one paragraph

## Quality checks
- Stages do not overlap and together run from first input to end use
- Margins use one measure throughout and name their source
- Every control point names the mechanism that gives it power
- The user's weak stages are stated plainly

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The industry primer chapter of a strategy study, where a team spends a week on expert calls drawing every step from raw input to end customer and tracing where the money changes hands.
- **Example request:** “Map the value chain for commercial HVAC in North America, from equipment makers to service contractors, and show where a regional distributor like us sits. Two competitor 10-Ks are attached.”
- **What the user still owns:** Deciding whether to move along the chain. The map shows where power sits; whether you can take it depends on capabilities and relationships only you can judge.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
