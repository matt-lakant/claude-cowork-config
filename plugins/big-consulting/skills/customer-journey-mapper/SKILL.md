# Customer Journey Mapper

## What this produces
A journey map for one journey and one customer type, a service blueprint layer showing internal processes and systems, and a ranked list of pain points to fix.

## Inputs to ask for
- The journey in scope, its start and end, and the customer type.
- Evidence: survey verbatims, contact reasons by stage, interview notes, process documents, drop-off or cycle-time data.
- Internal teams and systems that touch each step.
- If evidence is thin for a stage, mark it as assumed and list what to collect.

## Method
1. Define 5 to 8 stages from the customer's point of view, named with verbs ("find out claim status").
2. For each stage record: customer goal, actions, touchpoints and channels, what they feel (with evidence), and duration.
3. Add a service blueprint (the technique G. Lynn Shostack introduced): frontstage actions, backstage actions, support systems, and the line of visibility separating what the customer sees.
4. Mark pain points with evidence: contact volume at that stage, low scores, verbatims, handoffs, and waits.
5. Identify moments of truth: stages where outcome or emotion most affects loyalty.
6. Rank pain points by customer impact (volume x severity) and fix effort.

## Output format
1. Journey table: Stage | Goal | Actions | Touchpoints | Emotion | Evidence | Pain Points.
2. Blueprint table: Stage | Frontstage | Backstage | Systems | Handoffs.
3. Moments of truth (2 to 4).
4. Ranked fixes: Pain Point | Impact | Effort | Owner.

## Quality checks
- Stages are written in the customer's words.
- Every emotion and pain point cites evidence or is marked assumed.
- Handoffs between internal teams are all shown.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** Journey-mapping workshops, when a team walks one customer journey stage by stage, captures pain points on the wall, and adds the backstage processes behind each moment.
- **Example request:** “Map our commercial insurance claims journey from first notice of loss to payment. Here are 300 survey comments, contact reasons by stage, and our claims process document.”
- **What the user still owns:** Fixing the backstage. The map will show that the pain lives in three departments that do not report to service.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
