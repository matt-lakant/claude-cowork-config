# Hostile Q&A Rehearsal

## What this produces
A ranked question bank of 25 to 40 questions, drafted answers for the top 15, a backup-slide list, and an optional live drill.

## Inputs to ask for
- The deck or memo being presented (attach it)
- The room: each person's background and role (audit chair, former CFO, industry operator, investor representative); names are not required
- Known concerns from prior minutes or conversations
- Backup numbers and the model, if available
- Sensitive topics: past misses, undelivered commitments, figures that changed
If the room is unknown, use a standard board mix and say so.

## Method
1. Build personas from the room: the numbers director (assumptions, sensitivities), the risk director (downside, controls), the operator (capacity, who executes), the strategist (why this, why now, what else), the capital allocator (return versus other uses of the money), the governance director (why is this our decision, what are we not being told).
2. Generate 25 to 40 questions across personas, including follow-ups ("and if that volume assumption is 20% light?").
3. Include at least three questions about past performance or prior commitments, the ones executives skip in rehearsal.
4. Score each question: likelihood (1 to 5) x damage if answered badly (1 to 5). Rank by product.
5. For the top 15, draft answers in three parts: the direct answer in one sentence, one fact from the materials, a close that returns to the recommendation. Under 60 spoken words.
6. Where the materials cannot support an answer, write an honest holding answer with a date ("We have not sized that; we will bring it to the audit committee in March") and add it to the backup list.
7. List backup slides needed, and "do not say" traps: defensive openers, promises outside the plan, blame.
8. Offer drill mode: one question at a time, each reply graded on length, directness, and evidence.

## Output format
- Table: Rank | Persona | Question | Score | Draft answer | Evidence source | Backup needed?
- Do-not-say list
- Backup slide list

## Quality checks
- Every drafted answer cites the materials or is marked as a holding answer
- No answer commits to something outside the stated plan
- At least three past-performance questions are included
- No drafted answer exceeds 60 words

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The murder board before a major board or investor session, where the team plays the toughest directors and drills the executive until every answer is short, true, and backed by a number.
- **Example request:** “The board sees our ERP replacement ask Tuesday. The audit chair is a former Big Four partner and one director runs a competitor’s supply chain. Grill me.”
- **What the user still owns:** Candor. If rehearsal surfaces a question you cannot answer honestly, fix the plan or disclose the gap; a polished answer to a real problem rarely survives a good board.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
