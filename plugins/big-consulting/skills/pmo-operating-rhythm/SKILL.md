# PMO Operating Rhythm

## What this produces
A forum table, a weekly and monthly calendar, an escalation ladder with time limits, and an artifact list with owners and due times.

## Inputs to ask for
- Program structure: workstreams, leads, sponsor, steering committee members
- Current recurring meetings with attendees and duration
- Decision types that come up (scope, budget, dates, design, people)
- Time zones and existing reporting tools
If current meetings are unknown, ask each lead to list theirs.

## Method
1. Audit today's meetings: hours per week per leader, overlaps, and which ones produce a decision.
2. Design four tiers: workstream stand-up (weekly or daily), PMO sync (weekly: RAID, dependencies, milestones), steering committee (every 2 to 4 weeks, decisions only), quarterly value review.
3. For each forum set purpose, chair, attendees, inputs and when they are due, outputs, and the decisions it may make.
4. Build the escalation ladder: workstream to PMO within 2 business days, PMO to sponsor within 5, sponsor to steerco at the next meeting or by exception.
5. Set the reporting calendar, for example status due Thursday noon for a Friday PMO sync.
6. Create a decision log: decision, date, forum, owner, rationale.
7. Cut or merge any forum without decision rights.

## Output format
- Forum table: Forum | Purpose | Chair | Attendees | Cadence | Inputs due | Decisions allowed | Duration
- Calendar view of one month
- Escalation ladder with time limits
- Artifacts: Artifact | Owner | Due | Audience
- Meetings cut or merged, with hours saved

## Quality checks
- Every forum has decision rights or is cut
- No decision type is left without a forum
- Each artifact has one owner and a due time
- Leader meeting hours are lower than in the audit

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** PMO setup: designing forums, reporting calendar, and escalation path, usually after month one has turned into noise.
- **Example request:** “Our transformation has six workstreams and everyone’s in meetings all week. Here’s the current meeting list. Design a rhythm that gets decisions made.”
- **What the user still owns:** Chairing it. A rhythm holds only while the sponsor shows up and refuses to decide outside it.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
