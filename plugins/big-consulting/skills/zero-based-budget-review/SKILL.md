# Zero-Based Budget Review

## What this produces
Decision packages, a ranked list of funding increments, and a proposed budget against prior-year actuals, split into rate and consumption savings.

## Inputs to ask for
- Prior-year actuals and current budget by GL line for the cost center or category
- Headcount, contracts, and recurring commitments
- The services or outcomes this spend delivers, and any legal or regulatory requirements
- The cut target, if one exists
If outcomes are not stated, draft them from the GL lines and ask the owner to confirm before ranking.

## Method
1. Define cost packages as category by cost center (for example, Travel in Sales East).
2. For each package, split spend into rate (price per unit) and consumption (units used).
3. Build three levels: Minimum (required by law, contract, or safe operation), Current, Enhanced. Give each increment a cost and the outcome it buys.
4. Challenge every line with five questions: stop it, do it less often, lower the spec, source it cheaper, automate it.
5. Rank all increments across packages by outcome per dollar.
6. Fund down the ranked list to the target. Everything below the line is proposed unfunded.
7. Assign each package a budget owner and each category a cross-cutting category owner who sets the rules.

## Output format
- Package table: Package | Level | Cost | Outcome | Rate or consumption | Requirement cited
- Ranked increment list with a funding cut line
- Proposed budget versus baseline: total, rate savings, consumption savings
- Owners matrix: Package | Budget owner | Category owner

## Quality checks
- Every minimum level cites a requirement
- No increment is funded without a stated outcome
- Savings are measured against prior-year actuals
- Rate and consumption savings are not counted twice

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A zero-based budgeting wave: workshops with every budget owner, rebuilding each line from zero.
- **Example request:** “Rebuild our marketing budget from zero. Here’s last year’s actuals by GL line and the contract list. We need to take out 15% without killing pipeline.”
- **What the user still owns:** Holding the line in the second year, when the packages you cut come back as “urgent” requests.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
