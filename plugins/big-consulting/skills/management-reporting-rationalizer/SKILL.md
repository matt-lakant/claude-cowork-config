# Management Reporting Rationalizer

## What this produces
A report inventory with Keep, Merge, Automate, or Retire verdicts, a design for the standard monthly pack, and the hours returned to finance.

## Inputs to ask for
- The list of recurring reports: name, audience, frequency, preparer, hours to produce (attach spreadsheet or list)
- Sample reports, if possible (attach PDFs or spreadsheets)
- Any access or usage logs from the reporting tool
- Which decisions each executive forum makes
If hours or usage are unknown, send the user a short survey template to collect them.

## Method
1. Build the inventory and total the hours per month.
2. For each report, identify the decision it supports. A report that supports no named decision is a retire candidate.
3. Score usage (opened, discussed, acted on), decision value, and effort to produce, 1 to 5 each.
4. Find overlaps: reports showing the same metric with different definitions or cuts. These cause the conflicting-numbers problem.
5. Assign verdicts: Keep, Merge (into the standard pack), Automate (high value, high effort, stable format), Retire.
6. Design the standard pack: one page of headline KPIs, variance bridge, forecast outlook, then drill-down sections by function.

## Output format
- Inventory: Report | Audience | Decision supported | Usage | Value | Hours/month | Verdict
- Overlap list: Metric | Reports showing it | Definitions used
- Standard pack outline, maximum 15 pages
- Hours returned per month

## Quality checks
- Every Keep report names the decision it supports
- Conflicting metric definitions are resolved to one
- Hours returned are calculated from the inventory
- Retired reports list who must be told

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A reporting rationalization: consultants inventorying every report finance produces, surveying who reads them, and cutting the portfolio to a standard pack.
- **Example request:** “My FP&A team produces 60 recurring reports and complains they have no time for analysis. Here’s the list. What do we cut?”
- **What the user still owns:** Switching reports off. Announce a retirement date, then see who notices; that tells you more than any survey.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
