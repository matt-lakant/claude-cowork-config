# Adjacency Screen

## What this produces
A scored long list of 15 to 30 adjacent moves, a 2x2 of attractiveness against right to win, and a short case for the top three.

## Inputs to ask for
- A description of the core: customers, products, channels, capabilities, and revenue by line.
- Ideas already on the table, and any leadership has ruled out.
- Market data for candidate spaces. If missing, give labeled order-of-magnitude estimates.
- Hard constraints: capital, time to revenue, regulation.

## Method
1. Generate options in five directions: new segments, new geographies, new products for existing customers, new channels, and up or down the value chain. Tag each with its Ansoff quadrant.
2. Count what each move shares with the core: customers, channel, capabilities and cost structure, competitors, technology. Flag moves sharing fewer than 3 as high risk, in the spirit of Chris Zook's adjacency research in Beyond the Core.
3. Score attractiveness (1 to 5 each): market size, growth, profit margin, competitive intensity (reverse scored), and time to revenue.
4. Score right to win (1 to 5 each): existing customer access, capability fit, brand permission, cost position, and channel access.
5. Weight the axes equally unless told otherwise. Apply kill criteria before ranking: any move that breaches a hard constraint is removed and listed separately.
6. For the top three, write what would have to be true for the move to succeed and the cheapest test of the riskiest condition.

## Output format
1. Long list table: Move | Ansoff Quadrant | Shared Dimensions (of 5) | Attractiveness | Right to Win | Total | Status.
2. Which moves sit in each quadrant of the 2x2.
3. Top three: what would have to be true, riskiest assumption, first test with cost and time.
4. Removed moves and the constraint each breached.

## Quality checks
- Every score has a one-line rationale.
- At least one option comes from each of the five directions.
- No top-three move lacks a defined first test.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The growth-options phase: a team generates forty adjacent moves, scores attractiveness and right to win, and brings three to the steering committee.
- **Example request:** “We sell maintenance services to mid-size manufacturers in the Midwest. Give me a screened list of adjacent growth moves and tell me which three deserve a real look next quarter.”
- **What the user still owns:** Honest scoring of your own right to win. Every leadership team rates its capabilities a point too high, and the screen is only as good as that self-assessment.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
