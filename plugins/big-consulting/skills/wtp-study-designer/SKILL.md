# Willingness-to-Pay Study Designer

## What this produces
A design memo, a field-ready survey instrument, and an analysis plan that turns results into a price decision.

## Inputs to ask for
- The decision the study informs (price point, tiers, feature trade-offs, increase tolerance)
- Target respondents: segments, roles, realistic reachable sample
- Current price, competitor prices, candidate price range
- Feature list if packaging is in scope
- Win/loss records and past price changes with their volume effect
- If the decision is vague, restate it as one question first

## Method
1. Match method to decision. Price range for a new concept: Van Westendorp (four questions: too cheap, a bargain, getting expensive, too expensive). Demand at specific prices: Gabor-Granger. Price against features and competitors: choice-based conjoint. Feature importance ranking for packaging: MaxDiff.
2. Set sample size. Conjoint and MaxDiff generally need a few hundred completes per segment you intend to read separately; if the user cannot reach that, downgrade the method and say why.
3. Write screeners confirming the respondent influences the purchase.
4. Write the instrument. For conjoint, limit to 4 to 6 attributes with 2 to 5 levels each, include a none option, and keep price levels realistic.
5. Plan bias controls: randomized price order, attention checks, and calibration to real transactions, since stated willingness to pay usually runs high.
6. Specify outputs: acceptable range, demand curve, revenue-maximizing price, attribute importance, simulated share.
7. Pair the survey with a behavioral check (win rate by quoted price band) where data exists.

## Output format
- Design memo (under 400 words): decision, method, sample, timeline, risks
- Full survey instrument, numbered questions
- Analysis plan table: Output | Calculation | Decision it informs

## Quality checks
- Rejected methods are named with reasons
- Sample plan is achievable given what the user said about access
- No leading question wording
- The plan states how stated results will be discounted or calibrated

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The research design phase: scoping the pricing survey, picking the method, and writing the instrument before a vendor fields it.
- **Example request:** “We’re considering a premium support tier between USD 15K and USD 40K a year. Design the study we’d give a research vendor to find the right price.”
- **What the user still owns:** Getting real buyers to answer, and deciding whether to trust the survey when it disagrees with what your best reps hear in the room.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
