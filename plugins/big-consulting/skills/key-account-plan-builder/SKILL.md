# Key Account Plan Builder

## What this produces
A four-page plan for one customer: where the account stands, who matters, where growth sits, and actions by quarter.

## Inputs to ask for
- Three years of revenue and margin by product, contracts, and renewal dates
- The customer's annual report, earnings calls, and press releases (attach PDFs)
- Contacts known, their roles, and relationship strength
- Products or services the account does not buy yet
- Recent service issues and competitive threats
- If relationship data is thin, map from the org chart and mark roles unknown

## Method
1. Account economics: revenue trend, margin trend, cost to serve, and share of the customer's estimated spend in your categories.
2. Customer priorities: pull stated goals and pressures from public sources and tie each to something you can affect.
3. Buying-center map: for each person, record role in decisions (economic buyer, user, technical evaluator, blocker, sponsor), stance toward you, and relationship owner. Name the gaps.
4. Whitespace: products not bought, sites or divisions not served, and competitor-held spend. Size each opportunity and rate probability.
5. Value hypotheses: for the top three opportunities, one sentence each on the business result for the customer and the proof you have.
6. Risks: renewal exposure, single-threaded relationships, open issues.
7. Action plan: 12 months by quarter, each action with an owner, date, and the relationship or opportunity it moves.

## Output format
- Account snapshot table: Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3
- Relationship map: Name | Title | Decision role | Stance | Owner | Next step
- Whitespace table: Opportunity | Size $ | Probability | Value hypothesis
- Risk list and 12-month action plan table

## Quality checks
- Customer priorities cite the source document
- At least one gap in the buying center is named
- Every action has an owner and a date
- Revenue targets reconcile to the whitespace sizing

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The key-account program: workshops with each account team to build a plan per strategic customer.
- **Example request:** “Build an account plan for our largest health-system customer. Here are three years of sales, their annual report, and our contact list. Renewal is in 14 months.”
- **What the user still owns:** The executive-to-executive relationship, and the honest call on whether this account deserves the investment you are about to make in it.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
