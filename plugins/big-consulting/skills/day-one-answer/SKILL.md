# Day-One Answer

## What this produces
A one-page early answer with a confidence level and the evidence each claim still needs, revised weekly.

## Inputs to ask for
- The key question
- What the user and their team already believe the answer is, and why
- Any facts, interview notes, or analyses completed so far (attach)
If the user has no view, draft the most plausible answer and say so.

## Method
1. Write the governing answer in one sentence a decision owner could say yes or no to, with a number and an action.
2. Structure it as Situation, Complication, Resolution, a compressed form of the Situation-Complication-Question-Answer opening in Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle: what is stable, what changed, what we should do.
3. Write three or four supporting claims that are jointly sufficient: if all were true, the answer follows.
4. For each claim, label evidence status: Have (fact in hand), Need (analysis planned), or Against (evidence currently contradicts it).
5. Assign a confidence to the overall answer (Low, Medium, High) based on the weakest necessary claim.
6. Write what would change our mind: the specific result for each claim that would flip or reshape the answer.
7. List the next two weeks of work: only what tests Need and Against claims.

## Output format
- Governing answer (one sentence, bold)
- Situation / Complication / Resolution (one sentence each)
- Claims table: Claim | Evidence status | Evidence so far | What would change it
- Confidence level with one-line reason
- Next two weeks: numbered list, maximum five items
Hard limit: one page. Mark the page "Working hypothesis, dated [today]".

## Quality checks
- The answer is specific enough to be wrong
- Claims are collectively sufficient for the answer
- Every Against item is shown, not hidden
- Confidence matches the weakest claim, not the average

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The partner’s habit of writing the answer on day one as a storyline of testable claims, so every later analysis either proves it or changes it.
- **Example request:** “We’re two days into looking at why our onboarding takes 40 days. Write the day-one answer so the team knows what we’re trying to prove or kill.”
- **What the user still owns:** Holding the answer loosely. An early hypothesis is a tool for speed, and the leader who defends it past the evidence turns it into a bias.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
