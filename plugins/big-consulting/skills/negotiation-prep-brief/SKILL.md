# Negotiation Prep Brief

## What this produces
A two-page negotiation brief: facts, both sides' alternatives, the zone of agreement, positions, concessions, and the agenda.

## Inputs to ask for
- The current contract, spend history, and the supplier's proposal or price increase request
- Alternatives available: other qualified suppliers, bids, insourcing, demand reduction
- The supplier's dependence on this business, capacity, financial health
- Must-haves and nice-to-haves beyond price
If the user has no alternative, say so plainly and build the plan around creating one.

## Method
1. Build the fact pack: spend trend, price history against relevant indices, performance record.
2. Define your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement, from Fisher and Ury's *Getting to Yes*) and cost it. Estimate the supplier's BATNA: what losing this business costs them.
3. Set the walk-away point from your BATNA, the target from should-cost or market evidence, and the opening beyond the target with a rationale you can defend.
4. Map the zone of possible agreement between your walk-away and the supplier's likely floor. If there is none, the plan is to improve the BATNA first.
5. List tradeable variables (volume, term, payment terms, forecast sharing, specification), and rank them by value to each side. Plan concessions that cost you little and are worth more to them.
6. Anticipate their three strongest arguments with answers.

## Output format
- Fact pack (one table)
- BATNA table: Party | Alternative | Cost of alternative
- Positions: Opening | Target | Walk-away, each with rationale
- Concession plan: Variable | Value to us | Value to them | Give in exchange for
- Their likely arguments and our responses

## Quality checks
- The walk-away point is derived from the costed BATNA
- Every concession has something asked in return

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A senior sourcing lead’s preparation before a big renewal: fact pack, walk-away point, the other side’s pressures, concessions, and the opening.
- **Example request:** “Our freight carrier wants 12% more at renewal next month. Here’s the contract and two years of spend. Prep me for the negotiation.”
- **What the user still owns:** Walking away when the number crosses the line. The brief sets the walk-away point, and only you can hold it in the room.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
