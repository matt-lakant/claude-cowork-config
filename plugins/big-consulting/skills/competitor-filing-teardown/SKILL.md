# Competitor Filing Teardown

## What this produces
A profile with a financial recast, a "what they say vs what they fund" test, signal changes over time, and predicted moves with confidence levels.

## Inputs to ask for
- Last three 10-Ks or annual reports and latest 10-Qs (attach PDFs)
- Four to eight quarters of earnings call transcripts
- Latest investor-day deck and proxy statement (DEF 14A)
- Field intelligence: lost deals, pricing, key hires
For a private competitor, use press, job postings, and pricing pages, and estimate revenue as headcount x public peers' revenue per employee, labeled as an estimate.

## Method
1. Rebuild revenue, operating income, and margin by segment for three years from the segment note.
2. Recast the P&L to common size and run a DuPont or ROIC tree: are returns coming from margin, asset turns, or debt?
3. Follow the money: capex, R&D, acquisitions, and headcount by segment. Compare with stated priorities.
4. Track themes across transcripts: what management started stressing, what it stopped mentioning, and which analyst questions go unanswered.
5. Diff risk factors year over year. New risk factors are disclosed worries.
6. Read the proxy. The metrics behind executive bonuses show what leadership will chase.
7. List every non-GAAP adjustment management makes.
8. Predict 3 to 5 moves over 12 to 18 months and their likely response to the user's moves.

## Output format
- Snapshot: Metric | FY-2 | FY-1 | FY0 | Trend
- Segment table with margins
- Say vs fund: Stated priority | Spend evidence | Consistent (Y/N)
- Signal changes: bullets with quote and source
- Predicted moves: Move | Evidence | Confidence | Implication for us

## Quality checks
- Every claim cites a document and section or page
- Facts and inferences are labeled separately
- Fiscal years are aligned with the user's calendar
- Predictions follow from evidence in the profile

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The competitor profile workstream: an analyst reads three years of annual reports, earnings transcripts, and investor-day decks per rival, then writes a profile that mostly restates the rival’s own marketing.
- **Example request:** “Tear down our largest competitor from their last three 10-Ks and eight earnings calls. I want to know whether their push into managed services is real money or investor-day talk.”
- **What the user still owns:** The intelligence filings never hold: what their salespeople are telling your customers this quarter, and how hard you want to provoke them.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
