# Frontline SOP Writer

## What this produces
A one-procedure document: purpose, scope, steps, decision tables, quality checkpoints, escalation, and a test plan.

## Inputs to ask for
- Future-state map or current process map
- Observation notes on how top performers do the work, including their tricks
- Systems and screens used
- Regulatory or control requirements the SOP must satisfy
- The owner of the procedure
Without notes on top performers, watch one before drafting.

## Method
1. Build the steps from what the best performers actually do. Their shortcuts often belong in the standard.
2. One action per step, starting with a verb. Name the exact screen, field, or form.
3. Replace nested if-statements with a decision table.
4. Add a one-line "why" to any step people skip or get wrong.
5. Mark quality checkpoints and the control each satisfies.
6. Define escalation: who, how, and within what time.
7. Write at about an eighth-grade reading level. Target one to two pages; split longer procedures.
8. Test: a new hire performs the task from the SOP alone while someone watches. Revise wherever they hesitate.

## Output format
- Header: Title | Owner | Version | Effective date | Review date
- Purpose and scope (three sentences maximum)
- Numbered steps with screenshot placeholders
- Decision table: If | Then
- Checkpoints: Step | Check | Control reference
- Escalation: Situation | Contact | Timeframe
- Test plan: tester, date, observed hesitations

## Quality checks
- Every step is a single action
- The SOP reflects observed best practice
- Control requirements are mapped to specific steps
- A new-hire test is scheduled

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The procedure-writing phase after a redesign, where a team turns the new process into standard operating procedures that are often too long to use and written for auditors instead of the person doing the job.
- **Example request:** “Write the SOP for our returns authorization process. Here’s the future-state map and my notes from watching our two best reps handle returns.”
- **What the user still owns:** Making the SOP the way work is done, which means supervisors coaching to it and updating it when the floor finds a better way.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
