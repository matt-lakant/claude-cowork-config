# Job Architecture Builder

## What this produces
A family and level framework, leveling criteria for each level, a title-to-architecture mapping, and a list of mapping conflicts to resolve.

## Inputs to ask for
- Employee list with title, function, manager, grade or band, and pay (CSV or XLSX).
- Existing job descriptions or leveling guides, if any.
- The number of levels leadership will accept, if already decided.
- If job descriptions are missing, map from titles and reporting lines and flag low-confidence placements.

## Method
1. Group work into 6 to 12 job families by the nature of the work (for example, Finance, Operations, Customer Service), then sub-families.
2. Define separate tracks: individual contributor, manager, and executive. Senior experts reach high levels without managing people.
3. Write leveling criteria on five factors: scope of impact, complexity, autonomy, knowledge required, and leadership or influence. Each level must differ from the next on at least two factors.
4. Map every current title to family, sub-family, track, and level using duties and reporting line. Treat current titles as weak evidence.
5. Flag conflicts: same title at different levels, a manager at or below a direct report's level, pay outside the band implied by level.
6. Propose a standard title convention (family + level descriptor).

## Output format
1. Framework: Family | Sub-Family | Tracks | Levels.
2. Leveling grid: Level | Scope | Complexity | Autonomy | Knowledge | Leadership.
3. Mapping table: Current Title | Count | Family | Level | Confidence.
4. Conflicts and proposed titles.

## Quality checks
- Every employee maps to exactly one family and level.
- The IC track reaches at least the level of a first-line manager's manager.
- Low-confidence placements are listed for manager review.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A job architecture project, when consultants collapse hundreds of inconsistent titles into families and levels with leveling criteria, then map every employee.
- **Example request:** “We have 340 unique titles across 1,100 people after the acquisition. Build a job architecture and map everyone into it.”
- **What the user still owns:** The conversations when someone’s level drops. Mapping is analysis; telling a tenured employee their title changes is leadership.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
