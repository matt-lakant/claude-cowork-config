# Entrant and Substitute Scan

## What this produces
A ranked list of the ten most material threats, each with mechanism, traction evidence, time to impact, and the indicator that moves it from watch to act.

## Inputs to ask for
- The customer job and budget line the product serves, in the customer's words
- Newcomers the user has already heard about in the field
- Win/loss notes on deals lost to unfamiliar competitors
- Any funding-database export, trade press, or analyst notes (attach)
Without external data, work from public knowledge and date every traction claim.

## Method
1. Define the job and the budget it draws from. Substitutes compete for that budget even when they look nothing like the user's product.
2. Scan six sources: startups, adjacent incumbents, large platforms, customers insourcing, suppliers integrating forward, and technology substitutes (including AI tools that absorb part of the job).
3. For each: target segment, price-performance vs the user, foothold (low end or non-consumers first, per Clayton Christensen's disruption theory, or head-on at the best customers), traction signals (customers, funding, hiring, pricing), and barriers still ahead.
4. Score likelihood, impact, and speed on 1 to 5. Threat score = L x I x S.
5. Cut to ten and assign Watch, Respond, or Act.
6. Give each threat a leading indicator with a numeric trigger (for example: named in more than 3 lost deals a quarter).

## Output format
- Job definition (two sentences)
- Table: Threat | Type | Target segment | Mechanism | Traction | Barriers | L | I | S | Score | Action
- Triggers: Threat | Indicator | Threshold | Owner
- One paragraph on the threat most likely to be underestimated

## Quality checks
- Evidence and speculation are labeled, with dates
- Substitutes appear alongside same-category rivals
- Every threat has a measurable trigger
- The list stops at ten; the rest go to an appendix

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The “emerging threats” section of a strategy review, where a team compiles a long list of startups and adjacent players from news and funding databases, then struggles to say which could matter within three years.
- **Example request:** “Scan for new entrants and substitutes in commercial insurance claims administration. We are losing renewals to firms we had never heard of two years ago, and our win/loss notes are attached.”
- **What the user still owns:** How much to spend defending against a threat that is still small, and whether to partner with, buy, or ignore the entrant.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
