# Frontline Observation Protocol

## What this produces
An observation plan, consent script, and field log template, then a gap log of observed vs documented work with the reason for each gap.

## Inputs to ask for
- The documented process or SOP, if any
- Roles, headcount, sites, shifts, and volume by hour and month
- Known pain points
- After fieldwork: raw notes, timings, photos of workarounds (with permission)
With no documented process, log gaps against what managers describe.

## Method
1. Sample for variation: every role, each shift, peak and quiet periods, month-end, and mixed tenure (new hire, veteran, top and struggling performer). At least two 2 to 4 hour sessions per role.
2. Consent script: we study the process, not the person; nothing feeds performance reviews; they can stop anytime. Flag union or HR notice rules.
3. Ask "show me," never "tell me." Sit where the work happens and stay quiet for 30 minutes so watched behavior settles.
4. Timestamp every step: action, screen, people involved, waits, interruptions, rework, and workarounds (sticky notes, shadow spreadsheets).
5. Debrief for 10 minutes: What is hardest about your day? What do you do that is not written down? Where do you wait?
6. Tag each observed step matches, differs, or undocumented, and record why each difference exists. Workarounds are usually rational answers to a broken step.
7. Separate one-off findings from patterns seen across people and shifts.

## Output format
- Plan: Role | Site/shift | Tenure | Date | Duration | Why selected
- Consent script (under 120 words), field log columns
- Debrief questions
- Gap log: Step | Documented | Observed | Frequency | Why people do it | Keep, fix, or kill
- Top ten findings, each tied to log entries

## Quality checks
- The sample covers peak volume and every role
- Findings name roles, never individuals
- Every workaround has a recorded reason
- Nothing in the synthesis rests on documentation alone

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The discovery weeks of an operations engagement, when consultants sit beside frontline staff with stopwatches to learn how the work really gets done.
- **Example request:** “We’re redesigning claims intake at two sites. Plan a week of floor observation for our twelve processors, with the consent script and field sheet.”
- **What the user still owns:** Earning the trust that gets people to show you the real workarounds, and protecting them afterward. If one observation becomes a write-up, you will never see the real process again.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
