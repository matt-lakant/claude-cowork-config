# Ghost Deck Drafter

## What this produces
A 12 to 20 page ghost deck specification (title, chart, illustrative data per page), sponsor review questions, and an exhibit list ready to hand to a workplan.

## Inputs to ask for
- The key question and the decision it feeds
- Current hypotheses, however rough
- Audience and meeting date
- Available data sources and who owns each
- What the sponsor already believes and what their own boss will ask them
If there is no hypothesis, draft one from the key question and label it "straw man, replace freely."

## Method
1. Write the governing thought as a hypothesis, then a storyline of SCQA plus 3 to 5 key-line claims. If a day-one answer exists, start from it.
2. Draft each page: a hypothesis action title, and a chart chosen by message. Change over time: line or column. Ranking: horizontal bar. Part to whole: stacked bar. Bridge between two values: waterfall. Relationship: scatter. Specify axes, units, and dummy data labeled ILLUSTRATIVE.
3. Specify each chart tightly enough that an analyst could build it without asking: data series, cut, period, comparison, and the one number the title will quote.
4. Write a kill test per page: the result that would prove the title wrong. A page without one is a page the team will bend the data to fit.
5. Classify pages: main body (needed to decide), backup, or appendix. Keep the main body to 12 pages or fewer for an executive audience.
6. Write sponsor review questions per section: Is this the question you need answered? Would this chart change your mind? Which page is missing? Which would you tear out?
7. Hand off: list each main-body exhibit with its data need and likely source, for the workplan to assign owners and dates. Do not rebuild the workplan here.

## Output format
- Storyline block (governing thought plus key line)
- Table: Page | Hypothesis title | Chart type and axes | Series, cut, and period | Illustrative data | Kill test | Main body / backup / appendix
- Sponsor review questions by section
- Exhibit handoff list: Exhibit | Data needed | Likely source

## Quality checks
- Every page traces to one key-line claim
- Every page has a kill test
- Every dummy figure is labeled ILLUSTRATIVE
- The main body is 12 pages or fewer

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** Day three of an engagement, when the team sketches the final deck on blank pages before any analysis exists, each page carrying a hypothesis headline and a hand-drawn chart, then walks the sponsor through it to agree on the shape of the answer.
- **Example request:** “We owe the exec team a recommendation on outsourcing accounts payable in five weeks. Draft the ghost deck so I can walk the CFO through the shape of the answer on Monday.”
- **What the user still owns:** Treating the titles as hypotheses. The ghost deck is only honest if you rewrite a page when the data disagrees, and the team takes its cue from whether you do.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
