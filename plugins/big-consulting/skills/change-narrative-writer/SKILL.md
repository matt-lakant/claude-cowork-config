# Change Narrative Writer

## What this produces
A core change story, an audience impact matrix, a cascade timeline, and the four artifacts: leader remarks, manager talking points, FAQ, and written announcement.

## Inputs to ask for
- The decision or program and its timeline
- What changes for whom: roles, processes, locations, tools
- What is not changing
- Known employee concerns (survey results, skip-level notes)
- What is still undecided, and when it will be decided
- Legal and HR constraints (notice periods, works councils, union agreements) and who must review
If role eliminations are involved, confirm HR and counsel have reviewed timing before drafting any announcement.

## Method
1. Write the core story in five parts: where we are (honest, including what is hard), why we must change now (external facts, no blame), where we are going (a concrete picture), what it means for each group, and what we ask and what we will provide.
2. State plainly what is not changing. Rumors fill silence.
3. Segment audiences by impact: directly affected, process changed, indirectly affected, external (customers, suppliers) if relevant. For each, list the top three questions they will ask and the answer, or the date it will be answered.
4. Sequence the cascade: leaders aligned first; managers briefed 24 to 48 hours before employees; directly affected people told in person before any broad announcement; then the all-hands; then the written follow-up.
5. Draft leader remarks (about 450 words, three minutes spoken), manager talking points (one page, including what to say when they do not know), an FAQ (15 to 25 questions, including are there layoffs, will my pay change, and why believe it this time), and the written announcement.
6. Tone pass: remove euphemisms ("rightsizing," "optimizing our footprint"), remove promises leadership cannot keep, name the loss where there is loss.
7. Consistency pass: every number, date, and name identical across all artifacts.
8. Feedback loop: a manager question log and a two-week pulse survey, with a set date for FAQ updates.

## Output format
Core story (300 words maximum) | Audience matrix (Group, Impact, Top questions, Answers) | Cascade timeline (When, Who, Delivered by, Channel, Artifact) | Leader remarks | Manager talking points | FAQ | Open questions with decision dates.

## Quality checks
- Directly affected people hear first, in person
- The FAQ includes the hardest question employees will ask
- Undecided items are stated as undecided, with a date
- All artifacts are flagged for HR and legal review before release

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The change-story workshop where the leadership team argues out why the company is changing, and the communications team then cascades it into town-hall remarks, manager talking points, and an FAQ.
- **Example request:** “We’re consolidating three customer service sites into two over the next nine months. Write the change narrative and the cascade, starting with what the site leaders say on Monday.”
- **What the user still owns:** Being believed. The story is only as credible as the leader telling it and what employees watch leadership do in the 30 days after.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
