# Carve-Out Separation Plan

## What this produces
A perimeter statement, an entanglement map with a separation method per item, a contract consent list, standalone and stranded cost estimates, and a separation roadmap.

## Inputs to ask for
- Perimeter: products, sites, legal entities, and employees in scope
- Carve-out financials with shared cost allocations
- Contract list with assignment and change-of-control terms
- IT application inventory and data locations
- Target signing and close dates
If the perimeter is disputed, list the ambiguous items first and resolve them before sizing anything.

## Method
1. Define the perimeter and list ambiguous items: shared sites, split employees, shared customers and IP.
2. Map entanglement by area (people, applications, data, contracts, facilities, IP, legal entities, supply) and assign a method: transfer, clone, split, or cover with a TSA.
3. Contracts: flag those needing consent under assignment or change-of-control clauses, ranked by revenue or cost at stake.
4. Standalone cost: what the business will pay to replace each allocated service. Compare to the allocations in the carve-out financials; the difference moves EBITDA and should move the price.
5. Stranded costs: costs left at the parent, each with a removal plan and date.
6. One-time costs: IT separation, entity setup, relocation, rebranding, advisors.
7. Sequence three states: signing, close (running on TSAs), and full standalone. IT separation and legal entity setup usually set the critical path.

## Output format
- Perimeter statement with ambiguous-items list
- Table: Area | Item | Shared or dedicated | Separation method | Owner | Target date
- Contract consent table
- Cost table: allocated versus standalone by service, stranded costs, one-time costs
- Roadmap by state, with the critical path marked

## Quality checks
- Every shared item has a separation method
- Standalone cost is compared line by line with allocations
- Stranded costs have an owner at the parent

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The separation workstream in a sale or spin-off: untangling people, systems, contracts, and entities, and sizing standalone and stranded costs.
- **Example request:** “We’re selling our industrial coatings unit. Here are the carve-out financials and the app inventory. Build the separation plan and tell me what standalone costs the buyer will throw back at us.”
- **What the user still owns:** The people caught in the middle. Who goes with the business and who stays is personal for each of them, and those conversations are yours.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
