# Org Design Options Builder

## What this produces
Weighted design criteria, 3 to 4 structural options with a top-two-levels sketch each, a scored comparison, and the processes each option needs to work.

## Inputs to ask for
- The strategy or goals the reorg must serve, and the problem prompting it.
- Current org chart and headcount by team (attach HRIS export or chart).
- Known constraints: leaders who must stay, regulatory separation, budget, timing.
- If no strategy document exists, ask for the top three priorities and label criteria as provisional.

## Method
1. Write 5 to 8 design criteria from the strategy, each testable (for example, "one owner for the end-to-end customer order"). Weight them to 100 with the user.
2. Diagnose the current structure against the criteria, citing specific handoffs, duplicate roles, or gaps.
3. Generate distinct options from the main archetypes: functional, divisional (by product, geography, or customer), matrix, and front-back hybrid. Each must differ in its primary axis of grouping.
4. For each option, use Galbraith's Star Model to check fit beyond boxes: strategy, structure, processes, rewards, people.
5. Score each option 1 to 5 on every criterion, with a one-line reason.
6. Name the coordination mechanisms each option requires (shared KPIs, councils, service agreements) and what breaks if they are skipped.

## Output format
1. Criteria table: Criterion | Weight | Current State Evidence.
2. Option sketches: primary grouping, top two levels, what moves.
3. Scoring table: Criterion | Weight | Option A | B | C | D.
4. Tradeoffs and required mechanisms per option (under 6 bullets each).

## Quality checks
- No criterion is vague ("be agile" fails).
- Options differ in grouping axis; none is a relabeled current state.
- Scores have reasons, and weighted totals are recalculated.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The design phase of a restructuring, when a team turns strategy into design criteria, sketches three or four structures, and scores them with the executive team in a workshop.
- **Example request:** “Here’s our operations org chart and our three-year plan. Give me three structural options for combining field service and customer support, scored against what the strategy actually needs.”
- **What the user still owns:** Choosing who leads each box. The analysis ranks structures; the leaders you put in them decide whether the one you pick works.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
