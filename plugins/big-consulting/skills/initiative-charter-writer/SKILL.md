# Initiative Charter Writer

## What this produces
A one-page charter a sponsor can sign, plus a list of gaps that block signature.

## Inputs to ask for
- The business case, proposal, or kickoff notes
- Sponsor, initiative lead, and the line executive who will own the benefit
- Target dates, budget (one-time and ongoing run cost), known constraints
- KPIs affected, with current baselines
If the benefit owner is missing or is the project lead, flag it as the first gap.

## Method
1. Write the problem in two sentences with a number in it.
2. State the objective as metric, baseline, target, and date.
3. Set scope: 3 to 6 items in and out, covering units, sites, systems, processes.
4. Map each benefit to a KPI, a P&L line, an owner in the business, and a ramp start date.
5. List deliverables and 4 to 8 dated milestones, each with a definition of done.
6. Record decision rights: what the lead decides, what goes to the sponsor, what goes to steerco.
7. Name the top five risks and dependencies.
8. Write kill criteria: the evidence that would make the sponsor stop the initiative.

## Output format
- Header: Name | Sponsor | Lead | Benefit owner | Start | End
- Problem; objective
- In scope / out of scope
- Benefits table: Benefit | KPI | Baseline | Target | P&L line | Owner | Ramp start
- Milestones: Milestone | Date | Done means
- Budget: one-time | annual run cost
- Decision rights; risks and dependencies; kill criteria
- Gaps blocking signature

## Quality checks
- Benefit owners sit in the business, outside the project team
- Kill criteria exist and are observable
- Budget includes ongoing run cost
- Every milestone has a date and a definition of done

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** Mobilization week, when the PMO sends each charter back three times for missing benefits and owners.
- **Example request:** “Here’s the business case for moving customer onboarding to a new platform. Write the charter and tell me what’s missing before the sponsor signs.”
- **What the user still owns:** Getting the benefit owner to accept the number. An unowned benefit never reaches anyone’s budget.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
