# Change Impact Assessment

## What this produces
An impact grid by stakeholder group and dimension, a heat-ranked list of the most affected groups, and the readiness risks to address in the change plan.

## Inputs to ask for
- A description of the change: current state, future state, go-live date.
- Stakeholder groups with headcount and location.
- Other changes hitting the same groups in the same period.
- If the future state is not defined, stop and list the design decisions still open.

## Method
1. Describe the change for each group as specific before and after statements.
2. Rate impact 0 to 3 across dimensions: process, systems and tools, role and job content, reporting line, performance measures, pay, location, and required behaviors.
3. Total each group's score and weight by headcount.
4. Check saturation: count concurrent changes per group. Three or more in one quarter is a red flag.
5. Rate readiness per group (awareness, sponsor strength, past change history) as high, medium, or low.
6. Rank groups by impact times low readiness; these drive the change plan.

## Output format
1. Before and after table: Group | Headcount | Before | After.
2. Impact grid: Group | Process | Systems | Role | Reporting | Measures | Pay | Location | Behaviors | Total.
3. Saturation and readiness: Group | Concurrent Changes | Readiness.
4. Top 5 risk groups with the reason.

## Quality checks
- Every rating traces to a specific before and after statement.
- Pay and reporting-line changes are never rated 0 without confirmation.
- Groups are defined by what changes for them.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The impact assessment that precedes a change plan, when consultants grid every stakeholder group against what changes for them and rate the severity.
- **Example request:** “We’re moving 600 people onto a new ERP and consolidating two service centers in the same quarter. Assess the change impact by group before we write the rollout plan.”
- **What the user still owns:** Deciding whether to delay when saturation is too high. The grid will show it; only the executive can slow the program.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
