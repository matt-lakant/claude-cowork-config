# Board Memo Writer

## What this produces
A 2 to 4 page board memo plus appendix, with draft resolution language flagged for counsel.

## Inputs to ask for
- The ask and its type: decision, discussion, or information
- The proposal materials (deck, business case, model; attach them)
- Financials: investment, P&L impact, NPV or payback, funding source, link to approved plan
- Alternatives considered, and why they were set aside
- Top risks and mitigations
- Prior board minutes or discussion on the topic
- The company's board paper template, if one exists
If financials are missing, write the section with bracketed placeholders and list the exact figures needed.

## Method
1. Open with a purpose line naming the type and the exact ask in one sentence.
2. Write a 5 to 7 sentence summary: recommendation, reason, cost, return, main risk, timing. A director who reads only this can vote.
3. Background: only what a director needs, assuming they have forgotten last quarter. Cite prior board action by date.
4. Options: the recommendation, at least one real alternative, and the status quo, compared on the same criteria. Each alternative states why someone reasonable might prefer it.
5. Financials in one table: base-case assumptions stated, sensitivity on the two drivers that move the answer most, reconciliation to the approved plan.
6. Risks: top 3 to 5 with likelihood, impact, mitigation, owner. Include execution risk and the risk of not acting.
7. Governance: milestones, metrics, when this returns to the board, and the trigger for an early return (for example, cost above plan by 10%).
8. Draft resolution language marked FOR COUNSEL REVIEW.
9. Cut the body to 4 pages; move detail to the appendix.

## Output format
Purpose | Summary | Background | Options (table) | Financials (table) | Risks (table: Risk, Likelihood, Impact, Mitigation, Owner) | Governance and reporting | Draft resolution | Appendix list.

## Quality checks
- The ask appears in the first two sentences
- Every figure matches the inputs and carries its period and units
- No straw-man alternatives
- Every acronym is defined on first use

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The board pre-read a strategy team drafts with the CFO and general counsel over several rounds, trying to get a real decision onto four pages the directors will actually read.
- **Example request:** “Turn this business case for the USD 14M warehouse automation into a board memo for the November meeting. We’re asking for approval.”
- **What the user still owns:** The directors themselves. The memo cannot tell you which of them has already made up their mind, and the legal wording of any resolution belongs to your counsel.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
