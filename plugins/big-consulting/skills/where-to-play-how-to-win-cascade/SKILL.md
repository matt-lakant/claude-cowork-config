# Where-to-Play, How-to-Win Cascade

## What this produces
Three to five mutually exclusive possibilities, the conditions each needs to be true, a one-page recommended cascade, and tests for the conditions most in doubt.

## Inputs to ask for
- Current strategy documents, planning decks, or goals (attach).
- Revenue and margin by segment, product, channel, and geography.
- Any segmentation, adjacency, or competitor work, and who makes the call by when.

## Method
1. Use the five choices from Lafley and Martin's Playing to Win: winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, must-have capabilities, management systems.
2. Write 3 to 5 possibilities that differ in where to play (segments, geographies, channels, offers) and how to win (lower cost or differentiation). Each is a coherent story; none blends the others.
3. For each possibility, list conditions that would have to be true across five areas: industry, customer value, business model, capabilities, competitor reaction.
4. Ask the user which conditions they doubt most. These are barrier conditions. If the user is unavailable, flag the three with the weakest evidence.
5. For each barrier condition, design a test: the data or experiment, who runs it, and what result would kill the possibility.
6. Recommend one possibility based on the conditions already supported by evidence, and state which barrier conditions remain open.
7. Complete the cascade and check that each level supports the one above.

## Output format
1. Possibilities table: Possibility | Where to Play | How to Win | Barrier Conditions.
2. What would have to be true table: Condition | Area | Evidence Today | Confidence (High/Med/Low) | Test.
3. One-page cascade: five choices, each in 1 to 3 sentences.
4. What we will stop doing (at least 3 items).

## Quality checks
- Possibilities are mutually exclusive; no two share both where to play and how to win.
- "How to win" names a specific advantage over a named competitor type.
- The cascade includes explicit choices about what not to do.

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The strategy-choice workshops that narrow a list of options to one integrated set of choices, then test what would have to be true.
- **Example request:** “Our industrial distribution business is stuck at 3% growth. Here are the plan deck and segment P&Ls. Lay out the real strategic choices and tell me what would have to be true for each.”
- **What the user still owns:** Making the choice and living with what you give up. A cascade that says yes to everything is a budget, and only the executive can say no in the room.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
