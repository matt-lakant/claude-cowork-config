# Two-Uncertainty Scenario Builder

## What this produces
Four named futures with narratives and key variable values, a strategy stress test, the moves that pay off in all four, and a signpost dashboard.

## Inputs to ask for
- The focal decision and horizon (for example: build a second plant, 7 years)
- The current strategy or plan, one page
- Candidate uncertainties (reuse Trend-to-Implication output if available)
- Variables the business is most sensitive to
If the decision is vague, pin it down first. Scenarios without a decision become fiction.

## Method
1. State the focal question and horizon in one sentence.
2. Separate predetermined elements (true in every scenario) from critical uncertainties.
3. Rank uncertainties on impact and degree of uncertainty. Pick the top two that are independent; merge any pair that moves together.
4. Define each axis by two plausible, measurable end states at the horizon.
5. For each quadrant: a memorable name, a 150-word narrative of how the world got there, and values for the key variables.
6. Check that each scenario's variables can coexist.
7. Stress-test the current strategy in each world: revenue and margin direction, what breaks, what wins.
8. Sort responses: moves that pay off in all four, hedges against the worst, and options to buy now and exercise later.
9. Set 2 or 3 signposts per axis with thresholds.

## Output format
- Focal question and horizon
- 2x2 grid as a table with labeled axes
- Four scenarios: narrative plus Variable | Value at horizon
- Stress test: Scenario | Revenue | Margin | What breaks | What wins
- Moves: Move | Type | Cost | Trigger to scale
- Signposts: Axis | Indicator | Threshold | Current reading

## Quality checks
- The axes are independent and both matter to the decision
- At least one scenario is uncomfortable for the current strategy
- The four are not a best, base, and worst ranking in disguise
- Every signpost is observable and has a threshold

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** A scenario planning workshop: a team interviews executives, picks the two uncertainties that matter most, writes four future worlds, and tests the current strategy against each.
- **Example request:** “We are deciding whether to commit USD 60M to a second distribution center by 2028. Build four scenarios around the uncertainties that matter and stress-test the plan against each.”
- **What the user still owns:** The decision itself. Scenarios make the risk visible; the capital commitment and the accountability for it stay with you.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
