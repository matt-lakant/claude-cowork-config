# Deal Desk Reviewer

## What this produces
A one-page review: true deal economics, term-by-term risks, and a recommendation with a counterproposal.

## Inputs to ask for
- The quote or pricing sheet and the draft contract or order form (PDF or DOCX)
- The discount policy and approval matrix
- Unit cost or target margin for the products involved
- Customer history: prior pricing, payment behavior
- The rep's justification for the non-standard elements
- If the contract is missing, review economics only and say so

## Method
1. Compute deal economics over the full term: total contract value, net price per unit, discount versus list and versus target, pocket margin after rebates, free months, services thrown in, and payment terms cost.
2. Compare to policy. Name each element outside the matrix and who must approve it.
3. Review terms that carry hidden cost: extended payment terms, most-favored-customer clauses, price caps or frozen escalators, termination for convenience, unlimited usage, unusual service levels, and auto-renewal removed.
4. Price the non-standard terms in dollars where possible (carrying cost of payment terms, value of a frozen escalator over the term).
5. Check precedent: will this price become the reference for peers or the next renewal?
6. Build the counterproposal: keep the headline number if needed, and trade the concession for a give (term length, volume commitment, prepayment).

## Output format
- Deal summary: TCV | Net discount % | Pocket margin % | Policy gap
- Terms table: Clause | What it says | Standard | Cost or risk $ | Recommendation
- Recommendation: approve / approve with changes / reject, in two sentences
- Counterproposal: the exact changes to offer the customer

## Quality checks
- Economics cover the full contract term, not year one
- The counterproposal is specific enough to send
- The review states which approver the policy requires

## Notes from the source article
- **Replaces:** The deal desk analyst who checks a large or non-standard deal’s economics and terms before signature.
- **Example request:** “Here’s a three-year order form from our biggest distributor asking for 22 percent off, net 90, and a price freeze. Review it and tell me how to counter.”
- **What the user still owns:** The relationship call on a strategic account, including signing a deal the numbers argue against.
- When handing the deliverable back, name in one line the decision above that stays with the user.
